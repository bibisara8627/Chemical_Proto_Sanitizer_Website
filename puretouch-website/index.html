01 · About Product

A sanitizer we prepared ourselves

Product made by Bibisara Shaikh, Anushka Khaire and Payal Bandagar

This website presents a hand sanitizer made by students as a laboratory project. Everything shown here comes from our own photos and measurements. Details we have not yet confirmed are marked clearly.

Purpose

A liquid hand sanitizer for a proposed pack of about 100 ml, prepared and documented as a student project.

Appearance

Clear, colourless liquid with fine bubbles, as seen in the product photo.

Prepared in

A college chemistry laboratory using measuring cylinders and beakers.

Directions for use General guidance, to be confirmed by the team

  • Apply a small amount (approximately 2–3 ml) to dry hands. (To be confirmed)
  • Rub hands thoroughly covering palms, backs, and fingers until completely dry. (To be confirmed)
  • Allow to air-dry without wiping off. (To be confirmed)

Precautions & storage General guidance, to be confirmed by the team

  • For external use only. Keep away from eyes. Do not ingest. Keep out of reach of children.
  • Alcohol-based products are flammable: keep away from open flame, heat, and sparks.
  • Store in a cool place, tightly closed. Stop use if skin irritation occurs.

02 · Product Ingredients

Ingredient transparency

These are the exact quantities our team measured out for one batch. They are quantities used, not final concentrations.

Sum of measured quantities: 101.5 ml (75 ml Ethanol + 2 ml Glycerol + 4.5 ml Hydrogen Peroxide + 20 ml Distilled Water). We do not label the batch as exactly 100 ml until its final volume is measured.

Verification needed. The final formulation and alcohol concentration must be verified before any efficacy claim is made or the product is sold. Using 75 ml of ethanol does not mean the finished sanitizer is 75% alcohol, because that depends on the strength of the ethanol used and on how the ingredients combine.

Why These Ingredients?

Alcohol base + water

Ethanol is the main component in alcohol-based sanitizers, and distilled water adjusts the mixture's strength. The balance between the two is what determines the final alcohol concentration, which is still to be verified.

Glycerol + hydrogen peroxide

Glycerol is a common skin-friendly additive, and hydrogen peroxide appears in some published sanitizer recipes. Why our team chose these amounts is for the team to explain from its lab notes.

General roles only. They describe how these ingredients are typically used, not proof of how PureTouch performs.

What still needs to be recorded

Visual quality checks Proposed test

  • Appearance: colour and clarity of the finished liquid
  • Consistency: is it uniform, with no particles
  • Packaging: close the bottle, invert it, check for leaks
  • Final volume: measure it, since the supplied quantities add up to 101.5 ml

Easy to film. These show quality, not germ-killing ability.

Laboratory efficacy testing Proposed test

  • Alcohol concentration measured by a qualified facility
  • Antimicrobial testing under supervision
  • Skin-safety review before any sale

Only a qualified laboratory result can support a germ-killing claim. None exists yet.
